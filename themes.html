AI competition · Awaiting a fresh assessment

AI customers can move work quickly. The computing bills last for years.

AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic spend heavily on computing power, often under contracts that run for years. Their customers are far less tied down. Data from Ramp, a corporate-card company, shows more businesses changing AI providers, and a customer can move a task to a rival without closing its account. This story tracks that gap: revenue that can shift quickly against bills that last for years. Reported trouble raising money at OpenAI and a cautious credit forecast for Anthropic suggest investors are now asking how the gap gets paid for.

AI competition. AI customers can move work quickly. The computing bills last for years.

Useful AI Werks. Analysis by Codex and Persephone. Reviewed October 31, 2026. Synthetic narration.

AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic spend heavily on computing power, often under contracts that run for years. Their customers are far less tied down. Data from Ramp, a corporate-card company, shows more businesses changing AI providers, and a customer can move a task to a rival without closing its account. This story tracks that gap: revenue that can shift quickly against bills that last for years. Reported trouble raising money at OpenAI and a cautious credit forecast for Anthropic suggest investors are now asking how the gap gets paid for.

Three takeaways. A customer moves a task. The next job goes to a rival while the account stays open. Usage revenue follows. Metered charges reflect less use; prepaid commitments can delay the cash effect. The computing bill stays. Contracts signed for years keep running.

Why it matters. This is the risk hiding behind headline growth numbers. Total AI use can keep rising while one provider loses its most valuable work, so adoption says little about any single company’s health. The danger is a squeeze. A provider committed to years of computing costs needs steady revenue, and usage-based pricing exposes reduced consumption sooner than an account cancellation. Prepaid credits and minimum-spend commitments can delay the effect on cash receipts. Falling prices make it tighter, because customers can use more AI without paying much more. If raising money also gets harder, a provider has less time to win that work back. In the optimistic case, useful products keep customers in place and revenue grows enough to cover the bills. In the pessimistic case, work keeps moving while prices fall, leaving fixed bills and thinner income. The transaction samples show spending shifting while adoption or use grows; the financing reports add evidence of investor caution. They do not measure every provider’s workload retention.

Anthropic’s public filing to list on Nasdaq reports $16.3 billion in first-half revenue and major multiyear commitments for computing power. Most of its $80.7 billion first-half net loss came from accounting charges tied to its financing. Why it’s here. The cost side of the story: bills fixed for years while customers can move. Source: 1news, citing The Wall Street Journal.

AI can produce research faster than experts can check it

AI in science. AI can produce research faster than experts can check it

Useful AI Werks. Analysis by Codex and Persephone. Reviewed October 30, 2026. Synthetic narration.

AI labs want scientists using their models, and they are widening access through free credits and partnerships. They are also publishing AI-generated results in mathematics, computer science and physics. This story follows what happens next: every new result still needs specialists to verify it, reproduce it and explain it, and expert time does not scale the way AI output does.

Three takeaways. Generate a candidate. AI produces a proof, explanation or experiment. Make it checkable. Experts get the materials and controls to test it. Make it usable. The result is explained so others can build on it.

Why it matters. AI’s value in science depends on results people can trust and build on, and volume alone provides neither. If output grows faster than checking capacity, unverified claims pile up and expert attention becomes the scarce resource. That raises the importance of the institutions that fund and review research, because they influence which tools scientists use and which claims get examined. It also makes openness a practical matter: public data and methods let outside specialists scrutinize a result, while withheld materials restrict who can examine it. Shared datasets and software are also the part of a free-credit program that can outlast the credits.

How the story developed. First labs widen access, then the results arrive, and the checking work becomes visible.

Google pledged access to its most advanced models for up to 200 European researchers, plus $3 million in computing credits, in support of the EU’s RAISE research initiative. Why it’s here. The first step in the story: labs competing to put their tools in scientists’ hands. Source: Google.

A system called VERID checks an agent’s actions against a live model of the lab, while humans approve sequences, verify protocols and supervise runs. Why it’s here. Even in Google’s own prototypes, people remain the checking layer. Source: Google Research.

Three takeaways. Price the extras. Search, retrieval and waiting time are billed or borne separately. Connect the parts. Information can get lost between components. Count the finished work. Include setup, retries and correction in the cost.

Why it matters. As model calls get cheaper, engineering and operating costs can become a larger share of the bill. Buyers comparing only model prices can pick the cheaper option on paper and pay more in practice. Providers, meanwhile, get a new product to sell: taking the integration work off a customer’s hands. Open, swappable parts let a customer change suppliers, but the customer still has to connect them and fix what breaks between them. In a voice system, for instance, a transcript can keep the words and lose the tone or uncertainty that should change the response.
