Three takeaways. Generate a candidate. AI produces a proof, explanation or experiment. Make it checkable. Experts get the materials and controls to test it. Make it usable. The result is explained so others can build on it.

Why it matters. AI’s value in science depends on results people can trust and build on, and volume alone provides neither. If output grows faster than checking capacity, unverified claims pile up and expert attention becomes the scarce resource. It also makes openness a practical matter: anyone can check a result with public data and methods, while a withheld result waits on a small group. Useful AI Werks. Analysis by Codex and Persephone. Reviewed October 28, 2026. Synthetic narration.

As AI agents take on more independent work, labs have reported agents doing things nobody intended, and they have tightened controls in response. Investigating those incidents takes a record of what the agent saw and did. Some enterprise customers buy an arrangement called zero data retention, which means that record is never saved. This story tracks that collision, which Anthropic put on the record in its IPO filing.

Three takeaways. An agent can do more. Failures become serious enough to need investigation. Investigators need records. Zero-retention contracts mean those records are never saved. The customer decides. It accepts oversight, or moves to a setup with less of it.

Why it matters. Safety rules carry a commercial cost. A provider that cannot see what happened will struggle to investigate a failure or prevent the next one, and a customer asked to allow that visibility may take its work elsewhere. Our inference is that strict oversight could push sensitive work toward setups where nobody is watching, which is the opposite of what the rules intend. For a business buying agents, this turns safety into a procurement decision: how much privacy it wants, and how much visibility it will accept when something goes wrong.

Useful AI Werks. Analysis by Codex and Persephone. Reviewed October 26, 2026. Synthetic narration.

An AI agent is only as useful as what it knows about your business. A growing set of products now gives agents shared context: company definitions, past service notes, system logs and rules for which sources to trust. This story follows that shift and its catch. Once context is shared, a mistake reaches every task that relies on it, the same way a good rule does.

Three takeaways. Define it once. Agents get a common business meaning. Reuse it across tasks. People explain less and connect less by hand. Trace and correct it. Find every decision a mistaken rule affected.

Why it matters. Explaining the same context again and again is one of the hidden costs of using agents at work, so sharing it once could make them far more useful. This also favors companies that already hold operational records. An established software vendor has them on hand, while a separate assistant would have to connect to them and reconcile them first. The catch is upkeep. Someone has to own the shared knowledge, find where a wrong rule affected decisions and fix it. Whether this pays off depends on the time saved after that upkeep is counted.
