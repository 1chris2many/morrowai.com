Applied Materials via SEC EDGAR

Applied Materials revenue rises 53% as memory equipment takes a larger share

Applied Materials reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $10.41 billion on November 12, up 53% year over year. Semiconductor Systems sales rose from $4.879 billion to $7.996 billion, with DRAM increasing from 28% to 33% of that segment. The company forecasts $11.1 billion in first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue, plus or minus $500 million.

Equipment purchases commit money before the resulting chips can earn it back. Growth in both the segment and DRAM’s share shows manufacturers buying more memory-making capacity, extending the spending cycle beyond GPU orders. It gives equipment suppliers current revenue while leaving chipmakers exposed if eventual demand or memory prices disappoint; these figures do not isolate HBM or prove AI customers’ returns.

Quarter ended October 25, 2026. Segment comparisons use the company’s recast prior-year figures.

Cisco Newsroom

Cisco raises hyperscaler AI infrastructure revenue outlook to $9 billion

Cisco reported $18.5 billion in fiscal first-quarter revenue, up 24% year over year, on November 12. Networking product revenue rose 43%, and the company raised its fiscal 2027 hyperscaler AI infrastructure revenue outlook to $9 billion, more than double fiscal 2026.

AI spending is reaching the networks that connect compute, giving another supplier a route into the buildout. But demand growth and pricing power are different questions: Cisco’s total GAAP gross margin fell from 65.5% to 63.7% even as revenue grew. That combination makes the profit captured from each additional sale worth watching; it does not establish that AI caused the margin decline.

Quarter ended October 24, 2026. The $9 billion figure is company guidance.

Ars Technica

FAA expands human-reviewed AI routing advice to all 22 US airspace centers

The FAA announced on November 10 that SMART would cover airspace managed by all 22 US air-route centers. Specialists validate its recommendations before local controllers decide whether to use them. The agency reports that its earlier rollout rerouted 180 flights, saving an average of 10 minutes and almost 80 gallons of fuel per flight; the controllers’ union says it was not involved in design or testing.

The savings depend on controllers receiving useful advice without an offsetting burden of checking it. A national rollout therefore tests the workload and trust needed to turn recommendations into action. The reported pilot savings alone do not establish that human-review cost at scale.

November 12 reporting on the November 10 expansion. Savings are FAA claims.

Google

Google pairs Gemini 4 Hydrogen with automatic model routing

Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12 at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. Its new Antigravity Orchestrator switches between models within a session; Google reports near-Carbon success rates on Terminal-Bench 4.0 while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster.

Google is separating expensive architectural reasoning from the repeated execution work that follows it, then putting the routing decision inside its own product. Customers could get more completed work from a subscription without choosing a model at every step, while Google controls which model consumes the capacity. The saving depends on cheaper execution avoiding extra rework; the reported benchmark does not establish total cost per accepted result, including human correction.

Google-reported benchmark results. Antigravity access is for paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers.

Meta announcement

Meta uses long-term purchase commitments to help finance cleaner data-center materials

Meta announced multi-year purchase agreements for low-carbon cement, near-zero-emission steel, electric trucking and recycled plastics. Its Sublime Systems agreement supports at least 180,000 tons of lower-carbon cement and, Meta says, enables Sublime's first commercial-scale plant while giving Meta priority access for its data centers. The agreements deliver Environmental Attribute Certificates: Meta pays for the environmental benefit, and the materials themselves may be used elsewhere.

New factories need committed buyers before they can scale. Meta is using future purchasing to help bring supply into existence, and the certificates let it fund that production before its own construction can use the output. The test is verified delivery. Contract values and realized emissions cuts are not disclosed, and the commitments don't establish cheaper data centers.

Company announcement, November 12, 2026.

Notion security disclosure

Notion says Fireworks breach exposed AI chat data from fewer than 1% of workspaces

Notion says a Fireworks AI security incident affected fewer than 1% of its workspaces. Its Insights feature sent stored AI prompts and responses to the vendor for analysis; Notion disconnected Fireworks on November 3. The company says data was retained at the endpoints it used despite a contractual zero-retention commitment.

Usage analytics creates another copy of the work an enterprise is trying to keep private. Measuring AI adoption can therefore increase the data exposed through a downstream vendor. The commercial risk sits in the gap between a retention promise for individual inference requests and the actual handling of data across the whole product.

Notion disclosure checked November 13; disconnection occurred November 3. The company’s investigation is ongoing.

Internet Licensing Collective announcement

Yahoo Scout becomes first commercial customer of publisher-led AI licensing collective

The Internet Licensing Collective announced Yahoo Scout as its first commercial licensee on November 12. The nonprofit offers a single agreement for participating publishers’ content while letting each publisher choose which uses and licensees it permits. It says payments will reflect content’s contribution to AI products. The RSL technical standard remains separate from the renamed organization.

One agreement reduces the negotiation burden that keeps small publishers out of direct AI deals. The harder problem then becomes deciding whose reporting improved an answer and how much it deserves. Specialist outlets could gain bargaining reach, while their actual returns depend on attribution and payout rules that a first customer has yet to validate at scale.

Company announcement; publisher income and the commercial terms of Yahoo’s agreement are not established by this launch.
