Anthropic

Anthropic brings research credits to scientific funders and institutions

Anthropic announced an expansion of its free Claude-credit program across 20 scientific organizations and research groups. Partners include the Simons Foundation, the US National Science Foundation and the Wellcome Sanger Institute.

Distributing credits through established funders can put AI access inside the process researchers already use to finance experiments. Shared datasets and software could spread the benefit beyond each grant, while workflows built around one provider create a future dependency. The lasting value depends on what remains usable after the free credits run out.

Announced October 28.

Microsoft

Microsoft’s spending forecast reflects delayed capacity, even as Azure grows 48%

Microsoft reported 48% Azure revenue growth and $18.6 billion in quarterly free cash flow. On its October 28 earnings call, management put capital expenditure at $45.2 billion, including finance leases, and forecast about $170 billion for calendar 2026 after some third-party capacity deliveries slipped.

A lower near-term spending figure can reflect equipment arriving later, without customers wanting less AI. Delays preserve cash temporarily but can also postpone the capacity needed to earn revenue. Microsoft’s positive cash flow also shows why growing infrastructure spending creates different financing pressures across operators.

Fiscal first-quarter results reported October 28.

TechCrunch

OpenAI accounts for nearly 11% of Microsoft’s quarterly revenue

Microsoft’s September-quarter filing reports $10 billion in revenue from its commercial arrangements with OpenAI, including revenue sharing. OpenAI also owed Microsoft $7.2 billion at quarter-end, up from $6 billion in June.

Microsoft is both an investor in OpenAI and a supplier dependent on its purchases. That links the value of its investment and part of its sales to the same counterparty: diversification among AI products does not necessarily diversify the customer paying for the infrastructure. The receivables balance makes cash collection worth watching, but does not establish overdue bills.

Filing reported October 28.

Samsung

Samsung says AI memory demand is pushing up prices across applications

Samsung reported quarterly revenue of KRW195.3 trillion and operating profit of KRW107.4 trillion. The company said server demand and constrained capacity lifted memory prices across applications; it expects AI demand to outstrip supply in 2027 and plans to secure multi-year contracts.

AI operators’ expansion spending can become a supplier’s earnings before those operators recover their investment. Longer memory contracts may protect access to scarce components while committing buyers further in advance. That shifts some demand risk toward the buyer if expected workloads arrive late.

Results announced October 29 for the quarter ended September 30.

Sandisk (SEC filing via Groowe)

Sandisk’s data-center sales grow 61% from the previous quarter

Sandisk reported $11.031 billion in fiscal first-quarter revenue, up 23% sequentially. Data-center sales rose 61% to $4.802 billion while consumer sales fell 29%; the company forecast $12.2–12.6 billion in revenue for the next quarter.

The split shows why a weak consumer market can coexist with rapid growth at a storage supplier. Infrastructure buyers are becoming a larger source of revenue, changing which customers drive production priorities. The commercial question is how durable that demand remains after the current buildout, rather than whether consumer spending recovers.

Announced October 29; fiscal quarter ended October 2.
