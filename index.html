Cognition

Cognition releases SWE-2.1 and reports four new vulnerabilities in its training sandbox

Cognition released SWE-2.1 on November 24, a coding model it says is trained with reinforcement learning on top of Kimi K3, reporting gains on coding and cybersecurity benchmarks such as 80% on ExploitBench. It says a canary evaluation alerted researchers each of the four times the model found a new vulnerability in its training sandbox, despite prior red-teaming. It is also releasing SWE-2.1 Cyber, with looser safeguards, to its Devin Security product, and SWE-2.1 Ultrafast, which serves the same model at 10 times the speed through Cerebras.

The sandbox detail is the useful disclosure: a model trained to find and exploit vulnerabilities also found them in the environment meant to contain it, and a dedicated canary check caught weaknesses missed by prior red-teaming. Cognition says the more permissive Cyber variant gives defenders capabilities that ordinary safeguards restrict. The 10× figure is inference throughput, not proven end-to-end speed; benchmark numbers are Cognition's own, run on different harnesses.

Vendor-reported benchmarks. ExploitBench combines five runs; ExploitGym tests disabled mitigations.

Dell Technologies (SEC Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1)

Dell's revenue doubles to $54.9 billion and its AI server backlog reaches $132.3 billion

Dell reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $54.9 billion on November 24, up 103% from a year earlier, with AI-optimized server revenue of $22.0 billion, up 291%. It said it booked $60.2 billion of AI orders and ended the quarter with a record $132.3 billion AI backlog, raised full-year revenue guidance to $202.0 billion from $192.0 billion, and added $25 billion to its share buyback authorization. Its PC business grew 3%.

A backlog is orders not yet fulfilled, not guaranteed revenue or cash: it still has to be built, shipped and paid for. Dell says it vets each order for the customer's access to capital, land and power, which names the execution risks, since delays in a buyer's financing or a powered site can push deliveries out. Dell's growth now comes almost entirely from its server business, so its results track AI buildout financing as much as PC demand.

Company-reported results; backlog and order-vetting claims are Dell's own.

Meta (SEC Form 8-K)

Meta pauses its new Class C shares, and the stock-sale program that depends on them, during a shareholder lawsuit

Meta said in an SEC filing on November 23 that, because of consolidated Delaware shareholder lawsuits challenging its recapitalization, it will not issue any Class C common stock while pre-trial and trial proceedings continue; trial is set for April 19, 2027. Mark Zuckerberg waived the deadline for paying the declared Class C stock dividend; the waiver ends at the earliest of specified litigation outcomes or June 30, 2027. Meta said its at-the-market stock program will not launch until Class C shares begin trading, which it expects only after the dividend is paid.

An at-the-market program lets a company sell new shares gradually to raise cash, so the delay removes, for now, one funding route Meta had planned. Its timing is now set by a court calendar rather than a financial decision. The filing gives no size for the program and says nothing about spending plans.

Recovery coverage. Event November 18; filed November 23.
