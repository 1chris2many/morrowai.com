NVIDIA — October 15, 2026

Adobe and NVIDIA expand GPU acceleration for creative tools

Adobe and NVIDIA announced expanded creative-tool integration on October 15, including RTX Spark optimizations for Photoshop, Premiere and Substance 3D and GPU-based processing for Frame.io. The companies report gains on selected workloads, while a Nemotron-powered Brand Concierge version is due for enterprise customers later this year.

Accelerating Frame.io's media processing and search could shorten the wait between uploading footage and locating usable clips.

TechCrunch

Anthropic proposes a share-funded public benefit fund

Anthropic announced on October 18 that it plans to dedicate 5% of its shares to a public benefit fund, subject to stockholder approval. Its board could add another 2% for each 50% increase in valuation above a set baseline. The fund will be a wholly owned LLC; governance details are still to come.

Equity sets aside a stake in future value; the amount ultimately distributed and who directs it remain important open questions.

TechCrunch, citing a bankruptcy court filing reported by Bloomberg

Disney plans Character.AI hires

Reported offers cover at least 60 employees. A competing bankruptcy bid could still change the deal.

Google Research — October 14, 2026

Google opens Deep Review for technical-document feedback

Google Research opened Deep Review to the public on October 14 after opt-in trials separate from conference peer review. It initially accepts PDF and plain-text files, with two free reviews a week and higher usage through the Gemini API. Google says uploads are kept for a year but are not used to train its models.

Section-by-section checks can surface inconsistencies before expert review; upload retention matters for teams handling confidential documents.

NVIDIA — October 16, 2026

Mercedes-Benz and NVIDIA extend their automated-driving partnership

Mercedes-Benz and NVIDIA said on October 16 they will extend their automated-driving partnership, combining MB.OS with NVIDIA's Hyperion computing and sensor platform, Alpamayo software and Halos safety system. The companies describe support for driver assistance and future Level 4 applications, without announcing a launch date for those applications.

The collaboration combines vehicle software, sensors and a separate safety system, making deployment depend on more than improvements to the driving model.
