TechCrunch

Anthropic launches Claude Sheets and Whiteboards; Meetings is still coming soon

Anthropic introduced Claude Sheets and Whiteboards in beta on paid plans and made Claude Data generally available. Shared artifacts and interactive connectors let teams work with Claude across documents and partner apps. Its November 10 announcement describes Claude Meetings as coming soon.

Moving a draft into another tool can leave the assistant behind as coworkers revise it. Keeping shared artifacts and connected apps in the same workspace could reduce repeated context handoffs, extending the assistant’s role beyond the first draft; that depends on teams granting appropriate access.

Availability follows Anthropic’s current announcement: Claude Meetings is coming soon.

Ars Technica, reporting on a Google DeepMind announcement

Google reports 96% kitting success in Gemini Robotics 2.5 factory pilot

Google says Apollo 2 robots using Gemini Robotics 2.5 achieved 96% success sorting automotive parts in a Mercedes-Benz proof-of-concept. It also released ASIMOV-Jailbreak to test robotic agents’ resistance to unsafe instructions.

Reusable robot models could reduce task-specific programming, making changing factory workflows more practical to automate. The pilot leaves failure-recovery costs and reliability across broader production work unresolved.

The result covers automotive kitting; it is not a general reliability rating.

TechCrunch

New York prepares RAISE Act oversight for January 2027

TechCrunch reports that New York opened its frontier AI developer registration portal on November 10. The RAISE Act’s requirements take effect January 1, 2027: large frontier developers must publish and follow safety frameworks, and covered developers must report critical incidents within 72 hours of determining or reasonably believing one occurred.

Requiring developers to follow their published safety frameworks gives regulators a basis to challenge gaps between a lab’s stated safeguards and its actual conduct. Incident reports also move information beyond the lab, although confidential reporting means the public will see less detail than the regulator.

Legal timing and requirements checked against New York’s statute and implementation guidance.

Interesting Engineering, reporting on OpenAI

OpenAI puts research-job stop controls in the model-serving layer

OpenAI says Control Room can cut a research job off from further model output within 35 seconds after a stop instruction. The job’s code can keep running; enforcement happens in the inference engines. At announcement, automatic refusal during a Control Room outage covered frontier research, with the rest still rolling out.

Stopping at the shared model-serving layer means a broken or altered experiment script cannot simply ignore the stop order. The trade-off is a central dependency: a Control Room outage also interrupts authorized research. Faster enforcement still depends on a monitor or person deciding that a job should stop.

Announced November 9; published in the November 11 edition. The 35-second interval excludes detection and decision time.

Ars Technica

Researchers find over 35,000 Grok Build apps accessible without sign-in

Claroc used discoverable app hostnames to find open apps, some holding business or personal information. It says public-link access was preselected when publishing; SpaceXSI says apps stay private until shared and employee accounts default to private.

A hard-to-guess address loses its protective value when DNS records reveal it. App builders still need access controls that restrict who can open sensitive material.

The mass exposure concerns platform sharing; separate generated-code tests covered only Claroc’s own apps.
