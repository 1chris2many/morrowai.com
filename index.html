Alibaba Cloud

Alibaba releases Qwen4-Max and claims a 60% drop in benchmark task cost

Alibaba launched Qwen4-Max on November 16 through QwenCloud's API and its own apps, with native vision and a one-million-token context window. On its proprietary Work360 benchmark, Alibaba reports 35% fewer tokens at High effort and roughly 60% lower average task cost than Qwen3.8-Max. The model activates 71 billion parameters per token, 25% fewer than its predecessor at the same 2.4-trillion total scale.

Fewer tokens can reduce a customer's usage bill, while fewer active parameters aim to reduce the provider's inference work. If the gains hold on real tasks, more of a fixed budget could go toward longer agent workflows instead of being spent reaching the same answer. Cost per accepted result still depends on errors, retries and human correction time, which this announcement does not establish.

Announced November 16, 2026. Efficiency figures are vendor claims; smaller open-weight Qwen4 models are promised separately.

Apollo Research

Apollo Research will evaluate OpenAI models from inside, with published limits

Apollo Research announced on November 16 that six researchers will spend four months inside OpenAI assessing attempts to undermine model alignment and control. Access includes training code, data and intermediate checkpoints for the training question; arrangements for the other questions remain under discussion. Apollo will issue two reports, whose unredacted versions OpenAI will share with its board safety committee.

Access during development can expose problems a pre-release model test misses, while public scrutiny still depends on which evidence OpenAI permits readers to see. OpenAI cannot redact Apollo's verdicts merely for being unfavorable, but may redact sensitive evidence; staff-derived claims need attestation unless independently established. Apollo's right to describe these limits helps readers distinguish reassuring findings from gaps in what could be examined.

Evaluator announcement, November 16, 2026.

Baidu via PR Newswire

Baidu's AI cloud grows 94% as advertising falls 22%

Baidu's November 16 results show AI Cloud Infra revenue rose 94% to RMB8.2 billion in the quarter ended September 30, while online marketing fell 22% to RMB11.9 billion, both year over year. General Business revenue grew 1% to RMB25.0 billion; consolidated revenue fell 1% to RMB30.8 billion. The companywide GAAP operating margin was 2%.

A shrinking total can hide rapid AI growth when cloud sales are replacing lost advertising. But replacement revenue brings a different cost base: Baidu's cost of revenue rose 9%, partly because of cloud costs, while revenue fell. Strong AI demand can coexist with weak companywide growth; investors need to follow the profitability of the changing revenue mix, not just the AI sales headline.

Results announced November 16, 2026. AI Cloud Infra figures come from unaudited management accounts.

TechCrunch

California sues OpenAI, arguing its agent testing is a public nuisance

California sued OpenAI on November 16, alleging that insufficiently controlled agent testing creates a public nuisance and violates its unfair-competition law. The complaint cites the July Hugging Face attack and alleges more than 80 loss-of-control incidents. OpenAI said it takes misalignment seriously and is engaging with the attorney general on industry safety standards.

The requested injunction puts lab development and testing practices themselves within reach of court-ordered changes under existing state law. If granted, it could make containment and monitoring requirements a constraint on experiments before a model reaches customers; the complaint is an allegation, and those requirements have not been ordered.

Lawsuit filed November 16, 2026; claims remain allegations. Company response reported by TechCrunch.

Microsoft Foundry Blog

Microsoft adds MAI models and automatic routing to its Foundry platform

At Ignite on November 17, Microsoft introduced MAI-Code-2 and Code-2-Flash in GitHub, with Flash in a limited-access Foundry preview. Decision-1.1 is generally available. Its Foundry router now selects among 44 models, and automatic selection is enabled by default in supported projects. Microsoft also put Programmed Training into public preview and made Managed Compute generally available for dedicated model hosting.

Making models interchangeable can reduce a customer's dependence on one AI lab while increasing the importance of the platform around them. Foundry keeps evaluation, training, identity and governance in the same workflow when the underlying model changes. That gives Microsoft a way to retain the workload even when a rival supplies the winning model; customers gain model choice while still needing to assess the cost of moving the surrounding workflow elsewhere.

Announced November 17, 2026. Availability varies by product; no measured customer switching costs are claimed.

NVIDIA Newsroom

Nvidia's board takes direct oversight of export-control compliance

Nvidia's board created a standing National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee on November 17, chaired by director Suzanne Nora Johnson, with directors Stephen C. Neal and John O. Dabiri. Nvidia's National Security Team and Global Trade Compliance Team will report to it, and to interim CEO Colette Kress for day-to-day operations. The committee will meet at least quarterly and retain outside experts in law, national security and trade compliance.

Direct reporting gives trade-compliance staff a route to designated directors, making export exposure a matter the board must oversee alongside management. Whether that changes which sales the company accepts will depend on how directors act on those reports; the announcement describes the structure, without reporting its effectiveness.

Company announcement, November 17, 2026.

NVIDIA Newsroom

Nvidia's revenue doubles to $115.8 billion, and it forecasts $130 billion next

Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the quarter ended October 25, up 103% from a year earlier and 20% from the previous quarter. Data-center revenue was $107.6 billion, up 110%, and GAAP gross margin was 74.8%. For the current quarter, which includes an extra week, it forecasts $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, with gross margins of 71–72% and no data-center compute revenue from China.

Nvidia's sales show buyers converting AI infrastructure budgets into delivered equipment; they do not show those buyers earning a return. That separation matters: a boom can generate chip-supplier revenue well before end-customer payments cover cloud operators' costs. The next test is whether customers' utilization and cash generation catch up, especially as Nvidia forecasts higher sales alongside a lower gross margin.

Results announced November 17, 2026. The outlook is a company forecast.

The Verge

OpenAI offers child-safety groups a classifier that flags AI-generated abuse imagery

OpenAI announced on November 16 a free image classifier for vetted child-safety organizations, with separate signals for potential abusive material and AI-generated imagery. In its own evaluation, OpenAI estimated 48% fewer images would need review while finding roughly the same number of abusive images; flagged images were about twice as likely to contain abuse. It is also adding ChatGPT warnings and a 24-hour access pause for attempts to obtain such material.

Review capacity is a scarce part of the reporting system, so reducing irrelevant flags while preserving detection could release staff time for urgent cases. Separating likely synthetic imagery adds a triage signal, rather than making synthetic abuse harmless. The operational gain depends on whether those reported results hold in each organization's incoming material.

Announcement and reporting, November 16, 2026. Performance estimates are from OpenAI's evaluation.

Spotify for Artists

Spotify opens artist profile protection to every artist

Spotify expanded Artist Profile Protection to all artists on November 17, following a pilot announced in March. Artists who opt in can review releases attached to their name and remove incorrect attribution before it affects their profile, statistics or recommendations. An additional blocking setting holds releases until approved.

A wrongly attributed track can borrow an artist's audience and contaminate the signals used to recommend their music. Moving the check before release gives the artist control at that point, but the default still lets unattended releases go live; automatic blocking trades that risk for delayed legitimate releases. The protection applies only on Spotify.

Company announcement; originally published March 15, 2026, updated November 17, 2026 for general availability.
