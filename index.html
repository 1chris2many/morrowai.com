Anthropic — October 2, 2026

Anthropic commits $100 million to train 10,000 deployment engineers

Anthropic has committed $100 million to a program aiming to train 10,000 deployment engineers by the end of 2027. Participants complete in-person exercises and a 12-week residency on a Claude project at their employer. The first graduates are expected in early 2027.

Employers nominate engineers who bring a specific project to the course, tying the training to work their organization intends to deliver.

Anthropic — October 1, 2026

Barclays expands its use of Claude across operations and client service

Anthropic says Barclays' knowledge assistant has reached more than 16,000 employees and handled over one million searches. Claude also helps process about 120,000 emails a day in Global Markets. Barclays aims for half its developers to use Claude Code by the end of 2026.

The rollout puts Claude into customer-support lookup, email routing and software development at the same bank.

NVIDIA blog — September 30, 2026

CoreWeave opens early access to NVIDIA Vera Rubin systems

CoreWeave announced Vera Rubin NVL72 availability for early-access cloud customers, with Cognition running production workloads on the systems. NVIDIA reports that Cognition's early SWE-2 tests reached up to 4.8 times the token throughput of a GB200 NVL72 baseline. CoreWeave also launched Forge for training, evaluating and improving models and agents.

The hardware is reaching production customers, but the reported speedup comes from one coding workload and does not establish the gain for other applications.

OpenAI — September 30, 2026

OpenAI says it disrupted a campaign to extract its models' hidden reasoning

OpenAI says it disrupted a July campaign to extract protected reasoning from its models. It links a core cluster to people associated with Moonshot AI, while saying it is unclear whether all operators belonged to one group. The September 30 report describes 16,000 attempted extractions during a two-day spike, not 16,000 successful breaches.

OpenAI says the operators manipulated model interactions without accessing stored user conversations; the concern is reuse of extracted reasoning to train competing models.
