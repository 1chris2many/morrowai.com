Alphabet (SEC filings)

Alphabet closes a €6 billion bond sale with maturities out to 2045

Alphabet closed €6 billion of notes on November 5 after pricing them on November 2, according to its SEC filings: seven tranches maturing from 2028 to 2045, six with fixed coupons from 3.700% to 5.250% and one floating at three-month EURIBOR plus 0.50 percentage point. Alphabet says proceeds are for general corporate purposes, which may include repaying debt.

Together with its C$6 billion pricing on November 19, this offering shows Alphabet drawing on different currency markets and repayment horizons as capital spending expands. That broadens its funding options but does not show which investments the borrowed money will support or whether they will earn adequate returns; the filings specify general corporate purposes.

Recovery coverage. Priced November 2 and closed November 5, 2026. This is separate from the November 19 Canadian-dollar offering.

Calcalist (Ctech)

Amazon is close to buying Israeli AI startup Decart for up to $7.5 billion, Calcalist reports

Calcalist reported on November 18 that Amazon was near signing a Decart acquisition, with cash, stock and deferred payments potentially reaching nearly $7.5 billion. It reported five-year founder retention, a significant portion of the price payable in year five, and software that helps developers switch AI chips.

Amazon’s chip strategy also needs software customers can adopt. Decart’s reported portability work could help turn Trainium capacity into usable alternatives, while retaining the founders could preserve implementation expertise. The report supplies no measured switching benefit.

Recovery coverage. Reported negotiations: November 18, 2026. Disclosure: this article includes editorial contributions produced with Anthropic’s Claude models.

Apollo (press release)

Apollo and Goldman price about $44 billion of notes to fund SpaceX's Nvidia GPUs

Apollo and Goldman Sachs announced pricing of about $44 billion in senior and subordinated notes on November 20 to finance Nvidia GPUs and related equipment for SpaceX. The upsized transaction follows the roughly $40 billion commitment announced in October; leases are expected to begin with equipment delivery in the first half of 2027.

Pricing connects investor demand with a funding route for future compute, but available financing does not establish the return on that capacity. Apollo describes an asset-based structure; assessing who bears losses requires the collateral, lease-payment and recourse terms, which this release does not provide.

Recovery coverage. Pricing announcement: November 20, 2026; earlier commitment: October 26. Expected delivery and lease commencement: first half of 2027.

The Verge (reporting Bloomberg)

Jensen Huang's foundation committed $350 million to CoreWeave compute for grant recipients, Bloomberg reports

Jensen Huang's charitable foundation has committed $350 million to CoreWeave, by far its biggest gift last year, with more than $100 million already spent, according to Bloomberg as relayed by The Verge on November 20. A CoreWeave spokesperson said the money buys compute that the foundation's grant recipients use for research.

A grant that buys compute from a selected provider supports research and creates demand for that provider at the same time. Nvidia’s former CEO funding work on an Nvidia-backed customer’s infrastructure adds another relationship to examine when judging customer independence. The reported commitment alone does not establish its revenue treatment or fabricated demand.

Recovery coverage. Bloomberg reported November 19; The Verge relayed it November 20, 2026. Commitment and spending are distinct.

Loudoun County

Loudoun County pauses final votes on data center and substation applications for up to a year

Loudoun County's Board of Supervisors voted on November 17 to pause its public hearings and final votes on legislative data center and substation applications for up to 12 months, retroactive to September 15, while it rewrites its data center standards. Developers can still submit applications, staff will keep reviewing them and Planning Commission hearings continue; the pause can affect 33 active applications, allows exceptions for grid reliability or expiring statutory deadlines, and excludes the Route 28 Tax District and administrative approvals.

For affected proposals, the bottleneck moves to final approval while earlier review can continue. Waiting for new standards adds timing and redesign risk, with exemptions limiting how much capacity is actually delayed. The decision therefore changes the sequence and certainty of approvals more than the headline project count suggests.

Recovery coverage. Board action: November 17, 2026; county notice: November 18. Retroactive effective date: September 15.

Levent Alpöge; Ravi Vakil; TechCrunch

Mathematician proposes a Claude-assisted Riemann proof; rival proof faces reported problems

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge says he developed a proof of the Riemann hypothesis with Claude and released a Lean formalization before a readable explanation. TechCrunch reports that other mathematicians say it compiles. Ravi Vakil separately shared Alessandro Masullo’s proposed proof of the generalized hypothesis, then reported problems identified with help from AI models.

Formal checking can make an argument checkable before it is understandable; reviewers still need to examine its assumptions and whether the formal statement captures the intended theorem. AI is also helping researchers challenge a competing proposal. The opportunity is a faster cycle of constructing and checking arguments, while acceptance still depends on scrutiny.

Recovery coverage. Announcements and Vakil’s subsequent update: November 20, 2026. These are proposed proofs, not results independently verified by this publication. Disclosure: this article includes editorial contributions produced with Anthropic’s Claude models.

Microsoft (SEC filings)

Microsoft issues $25 billion of bonds in ten tranches out to 2066

Microsoft said in a November 4 SEC filing that it had issued $25 billion of notes under an October 29 underwriting agreement: ten tranches maturing from 2028 to 2066, with fixed coupons from 4.850% to 6.350% and two floating tranches at SOFR plus 0.35 and 0.53 percentage point. It says proceeds are for general corporate purposes, which can include capital expenditures, buybacks, acquisitions or repaying debt.

Debt reaching out to 2066 lets Microsoft raise cash now and spread repayment across decades. That can separate the timing of investment from operating cash generation, while adding interest obligations regardless of how those investments perform. Capital spending is one possible use; the filing does not earmark this borrowing for AI.

Recovery coverage. Underwriting agreement: October 29; issuance reported November 4, 2026.

Reuters (via Gulf Times)

SEC's Nvidia probe is examining potential accounting issues, Reuters reports

Reuters reported on November 20, citing two people briefed on the matter, that SEC enforcement staff are scrutinizing potential accounting issues at Nvidia, after The Wall Street Journal reported the probe without its focus. Reuters said it could not determine when the inquiry began or which issues are being examined, noted that investigations are not evidence of wrongdoing, and said the SEC declined to comment and Nvidia did not immediately respond.

For a company whose results set expectations for the whole AI supply chain, the question an accounting inquiry raises is revenue quality: how sales are recognized and to whom, not whether demand exists. Nothing reported so far says which numbers are in question, so the useful response is to watch what Nvidia discloses in its next filings rather than to fill the gap with guesses, including about its customer and geographic reporting.

Recovery coverage. Reuters report: November 20, 2026; Gulf Times print edition: November 22, page 3. The inquiry’s scope remains unknown.

The Verge; NPR; ICRC

UN talks on autonomous weapons end without a mandate to negotiate a binding treaty

Diplomats at UN talks in Geneva agreed early on November 21 to spend two years suggesting rules on lethal autonomous weapons that could be legally binding or optional, NPR reports, after the US proposed five years of non-binding guidelines and Russia delayed the agenda. The International Committee of the Red Cross, which had called for binding rules within a year, said it regretted the outcome and renewed its appeal.

Existing rules of war leave commanders accountable, but the talks did not settle specific common limits on autonomous targeting. The delay gives militaries more time to deploy systems before those limits are agreed, potentially making later restrictions harder to negotiate.

Recovery coverage. Talks concluded early November 21, 2026; ICRC response published the same day.
