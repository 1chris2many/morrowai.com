Rival AI chips are getting easier to try, if not yet easier to run

Many AI development tools are tuned for Nvidia’s chips, so testing an alternative can mean rewriting code. Google and Amazon are now removing that first hurdle, which gives buyers a real alternative to compare, even though a full move stays hard.

The Verge (Stevie Bonifield)

AMD's data-center sales nearly double in a record $13.4 billion quarter

AMD reported record third-quarter revenue of $13.4 billion, up 45% year over year, with data-center sales up 97% to $8.55 billion, about 64% of the total, on EPYC processors and the Instinct MI450 launch. Gaming revenue fell 40% to $773 million on lower semi-custom and graphics sales. AMD guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $17 billion, plus or minus 3%.

Data centers were already AMD's largest segment a year ago; this quarter widens the lead. Server and accelerator sales can now outweigh a sharp gaming downturn, making AMD's outlook more dependent on infrastructure spending. Its $17 billion fourth-quarter forecast will test whether the latest launches sustain that momentum.

Earnings reported November 3, 2026.

The Verge (Jess Weatherbed), citing Bloomberg

Anthropic's amended IPO filing pairs $17 billion in quarterly revenue with $895 billion in commitments

Anthropic's November 2 amended IPO filing reports $16.96 billion in third-quarter revenue and a $23.29 billion GAAP net loss, including a $21.94 billion fair-value charge on financing instruments; the operating loss was $567 million. The filing lists $895.4 billion in commitments, mostly compute and infrastructure, extending through 2045, with $69.8 billion due in the next 12 months.

The accounting charge explains most of the headline loss, but the payment schedule poses a separate financing challenge: capacity bought ahead of demand must earn revenue or be funded while it waits. The timing and flexibility matter more than comparing the entire commitment total with one quarter's sales; $213.8 billion covers conditional or terminable arrangements.

Filing amended November 2, 2026; reviewed for the November 4 edition.

Ars Technica (Jeremy Hsu)

Drone strikes push Russia toward underground data centers and state-reserved capacity

After Ukrainian drones damaged data centers run by Yandex, Sber and VK, state-owned Rostelecom has discussed underground facilities with Norilsk Nickel, according to Vedomosti; a regional governor has separately proposed sites in former coal mines. Both are proposals. A November 2 decree requires private data-center owners to reserve 10% of capacity for the state and lets a government commission, with FSB consent, authorize use of foreign facilities.

Concentrating computing inside national borders for sovereignty also concentrates the physical target. Russia now faces the choice every operator under threat faces: pay to harden a few sites, or spread capacity out, including abroad, which cuts against the sovereignty goal.

The Verge (Rani Molla)

Google's faster query growth exposes AI Search's monetization gap

Alphabet reported third-quarter Search and other revenue up 12% to $63.3 billion while query growth hit a five-year high. Management named currency effects as the largest drag on growth, with advertiser shifts and AI Mode's lower monetization also contributing. Alphabet is adding ads to AI Mode and AI Overviews and held its $195–205 billion companywide 2026 capital spending forecast.

More queries can add compute cost before they add proportionate ad revenue. Google must turn new AI usage into advertiser value while financing expansion. Cloud revenue, up 103% to $30.7 billion, offers another revenue path, but these results do not establish AI Search's unit economics.

Earnings reported October 28, 2026; November 4 reporting adds the Search monetization analysis.

The Verge, citing OpenAI

OpenAI hires former Army Secretary Dan Driscoll for national security role

OpenAI announced on November 2 that former US Army Secretary Dan Driscoll will serve as Senior Counselor for National Security Modernization, a new advisory role covering AI use in defense and adoption by US allies.

Defense procurement rewards knowing how requirements get written and how purchases move through the system. The hire gives OpenAI that knowledge in-house; it does not by itself mean any new contract or deployment.

OpenAI announced the appointment November 2, 2026.

TechCrunch (Aditya Mehta), citing The New York Times

Power delays push AI builders toward aircraft engines

Data centers are repurposing aircraft engines to avoid grid delays. ProEnergy's April 16 contract supplies Crusoe with thirteen 50-megawatt units, scheduled for delivery by summer 2027.

Owning generation puts the power schedule under the developer's control and adds a power plant to a balance sheet already carrying chips and buildings. The trade is paying more for capital and fuel to start earning on compute sooner; the contract shows planned capacity, not that the trade pays off.

Reported November 3, 2026; the supporting ProEnergy contract was announced April 16, 2026.
