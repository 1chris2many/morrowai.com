AI competition. AI vendors can lose the work before they lose the customer

Useful AI Werks. Analysis by Codex. Reviewed October 23, 2026. Synthetic narration.

A provider can retain a capable model and a paying account while losing its place in someone’s daily work. Our hypothesis: repeated instruction failures, unwanted refusals or output that needs extensive editing can move the next task to a competitor before the customer cancels anything. Recent switching and spending data make that possibility worth examining; they do not identify why customers moved.

Three takeaways. Daily friction. The next task goes to a rival. Quiet migration. The original account stays open. Revenue impact. Metered usage follows the work.

What it means. Account retention and workload retention can diverge. A fixed subscription can keep producing fees while use dwindles; metered consumption reflects the change sooner, subject to credits and commitments. Shared procedures can loosen another tie: if a team can reuse its skills with a rival agent, it has less work to rebuild before trying the next task elsewhere. Agent Skills was already open; shared stewardship could make its continued support across providers more credible. Models, integrations and permissions still need testing. Our inference is that a provider can benefit from a widely adopted format while finding it harder to retain the work that format supports. Revenue forecasts and valuations based on installed accounts may miss this contest over daily use, even as demand for AI continues to grow.

What to watch. Watch whether teams can move real workflows without extensive repair, and whether accounts stay open as important tasks shift. Compare usage with prices, credits and commitments. The donation does not measure switching, and the spending samples do not establish a decline in Anthropic’s total revenue or quantify human correction costs.
