AI evaluations. Who gets to challenge an AI result?

Useful AI Werks. Analysis by Codex. Reviewed October 16, 2026. Synthetic narration.

OpenAI has released mathematical proofs that can be checked while the producing model remains internal. Meta has published expert critiques alongside AI-assisted research. Anthropic is granting selected external evaluators deeper internal access. These are different ways to expose evidence, with different limits on who can investigate a result and what they can learn.

Three takeaways. Public artifact. Anyone can inspect the released result. Privileged access. Selected evaluators can probe more deeply. Right to report. Publication terms shape what others learn.

What it means. An inspectable artifact can establish something narrow and strong: a proof checks, or a manuscript contains a methodological weakness. It does not establish how reliably the model will perform on the next task. Privileged evaluation access can expose broader behavior, but the scope of access and rules for publishing findings become part of the assurance. For readers and buyers, the useful distinction is what a claim leaves open to challenge—and what remains inaccessible.

How the story developed. Earlier forecasting and survey tests exposed the importance of the chosen outcome. The newer releases expose another dependency: the evidence available to people who want to challenge the conclusion.

Source: Anthropic.

What to watch. Watch what METR actually publishes, any disputes over redactions, responses to the public research critiques and whether outsiders can reproduce results beyond the released examples. An access agreement is a route to evidence, not a safety finding.
