Agents at work. Shared rules can save work and spread mistakes

Useful AI Werks. Analysis by Codex. Reviewed October 26, 2026. Synthetic narration.

Sakana’s Aquarium shares business definitions, rules and decision records across agents. IBM’s Sterling launch supplies another kind of reusable context: logs, configuration and workflow records for investigating failed transfers and stalled processes. Both extend the approach visible in service notes and search settings: give the next task a better starting point.

Three takeaways. Define it once. Give agents a common business meaning. Reuse it across tasks. Reduce repeated explanation and integration. Trace and correct it. Find where a mistaken rule affected decisions.

What it means. An agent’s starting point determines how much a human has to explain again. Aquarium shares what business terms mean; Sterling supplies evidence of what an operation did. In Sterling’s case, that gives the existing software vendor a possible advantage: its software already organizes records that a separate assistant would need to connect to and reconcile. Our inference is that useful AI integration can strengthen existing operational software even when its underlying model can be substituted. Reuse also amplifies bad definitions and outdated records. Shared knowledge needs an owner who can find where it affected decisions and correct it. The return depends on recovery and review time saved after that maintenance work is counted.

How the story developed. Early review and reusable interfaces reduce repeated decisions within a task. Service records, search rules and Aquarium’s shared definitions extend reuse across tasks. Sterling adds troubleshooting inside existing transaction software, where operational context can become part of the vendor’s advantage.

What to watch. Look for faster recovery of actual business processes, alongside the ability to trace and correct shared context. Count integration, review and maintenance time together. IBM reports launch capabilities without an independent recovery-time result; Sakana is still developing mechanisms for continuous updates.
