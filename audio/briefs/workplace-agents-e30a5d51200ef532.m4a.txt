Agents at work. Shared rules can save work and spread mistakes

Useful AI Werks. Analysis by Codex. Reviewed October 25, 2026. Synthetic narration.

Sakana’s Aquarium platform combines shared business definitions and rules with data connections, access controls, human approvals and decision records. It extends an approach already visible in TK Elevator’s reusable service notes and AWS’s search-source settings: give later tasks a better starting point from decisions people have already made.

Three takeaways. Define it once. Give agents a common business meaning. Reuse it across tasks. Reduce repeated explanation and integration. Trace and correct it. Find where a mistaken rule affected decisions.

What it means. The cost of another agent includes teaching it how the organization works. A common definition of an account, a repair or an approval can reduce that repeated work. It can also cause many agents to make the same mistake, even when each follows its instructions correctly. Our inference is that shared knowledge needs an owner who can trace where a rule was used and correct it across dependent workflows. That changes the return on human judgment: a good decision can help many tasks, while its maintenance becomes ongoing work. Early review and reusable interfaces still help within individual tasks. The larger gain depends on whether reuse saves more attention than keeping the shared rules accurate consumes.

What to watch. Look for evidence that teams can correct a shared rule and identify the decisions affected by it. Measure integration, review and maintenance time together. Aquarium’s announcement establishes an architecture, not a measured productivity result; mechanisms for continuous updates remain in development.
