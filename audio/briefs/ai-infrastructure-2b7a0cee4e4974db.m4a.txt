Three takeaways. Secure future supply. Long lead times make expansion a forward commitment. Recover installed capacity. Limit the work lost when equipment fails. Count the whole fleet. Include spare hardware when comparing useful output.

What it means. Factory lead times limit how quickly an operator can buy its way out of a capacity shortage. Avoiding repeated work could therefore become more valuable while new equipment is unavailable. Meta uses spare hardware and checkpoints to recover quickly, but the capacity calculation needs care: many in-rack spares arise from fitting a 64-GPU job into a 72-GPU rack, while extra reserve racks are a separate commitment. The advantage depends on useful work preserved across the whole fleet, including spares, and the engineering required. Supply contracts address the next expansion; recovery can improve what an operator gets from equipment already installed.
