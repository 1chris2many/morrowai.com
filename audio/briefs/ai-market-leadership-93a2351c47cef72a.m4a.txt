AI competition. AI vendors can lose the work before they lose the customer

Useful AI Werks. Analysis by Codex. Reviewed October 26, 2026. Synthetic narration.

Recent switching and spending evidence shows that AI adoption can grow while business moves between providers. Anthropic’s IPO filing adds long-term compute commitments to that picture: customer use can change before the infrastructure bill does. Our hypothesis remains that repeated workflow friction can drive those moves; the data does not identify individual customers’ motives.

Three takeaways. Daily work moves. A customer tries the next task elsewhere. Usage revenue follows. The account can remain open. Compute commitments remain. Future bills can outlast workload loyalty.

What it means. A fixed subscription can keep producing fees after important tasks move elsewhere. Metered use can expose that loss sooner, subject to credits and customer commitments. A provider that has already promised years of compute payments can have less room to adjust its costs at the same speed. Our inference is that portable workflows shift more demand risk toward the provider holding those commitments: a growing AI market can still leave one vendor with an expensive mismatch between the work it expected and the work it keeps. Shared formats can help customers test rivals with less rebuilding. The durability question becomes how much daily work stays, and how flexibly capacity obligations can follow it.

What to watch. Watch whether important tasks move while accounts remain open, how usage changes after prices or credits change, and how capacity commitments can adjust. Separate financing-related accounting losses from operating cash use.
