Meta has published expert critiques alongside AI-assisted health research, including concerns about methods and interpretation. Anthropic is giving a small METR team deeper internal access and rights to publish its assessments. These developments extend the earlier story about testing forecasts, proofs and simulated opinions: the strength of a check also depends on what reviewers can inspect and report.

Three takeaways. Evidence. What supports the result?. Access. What can reviewers inspect?. Findings. Which concerns remain unresolved?.

What it means. Define a reliable result before choosing a test. Give reviewers the evidence and methods needed to challenge it, then track which concerns remain unresolved. Ask what information is excluded and who controls publication. For a product decision, measure the outcome users need and repeat the test on new data. An access agreement creates an opportunity for scrutiny; the findings still have to follow.
